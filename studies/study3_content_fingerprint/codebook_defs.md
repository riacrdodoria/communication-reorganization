# Codebook definitions — 6 validated taxonomies, 41 categories (code each episode by COUNTS)

Code each episode by counting how many utterances express each category. An utterance may be counted
in MULTIPLE taxonomies (they are different lenses) and may count for more than one category if it
genuinely performs more than one act. Within a taxonomy, assign each relevant utterance to its best-fit
category. Count 0 when absent. Transcripts are Brazilian Portuguese.

## CACS — Conversational Argument (Canary, Brossmann & Seibold 1987)
- arguable: makes an arguable claim — assertion/proposition/elaboration/justification/evidence/reasoning.
- converge: agreement / convergence-seeking — reinforcing, supporting, acknowledging another's argument.
- disagree: objection / challenge / disagreement-intrusion — denying or objecting to a claim.
- delim: delimitor — framing/qualifying/contextualizing/restricting the scope of an argument.
- nonarg: non-arguable — procedural/process talk, logistics, digression, non-argumentative utterances.

## Gunawardena IAM — knowledge construction phases (1997)
- iam1: sharing/comparing information — observations, opinions, statements, simple agreement.
- iam2: dissonance — identifying disagreement or inconsistency among ideas.
- iam3: negotiation / co-construction — negotiating meaning, proposing integration of ideas.
- iam4: testing/modification — testing a proposed synthesis against evidence/experience/logic.
- iam5: agreement/application — summarizing agreement, applying newly constructed knowledge.

## Mercer talk types (1995/2000)
- disput: disputational — disagreement, individualized decisions, short assertion/counter-assertion.
- cumul: cumulative — positive but uncritical building, repetitions, confirmations, elaborations.
- explor: exploratory — critical+constructive engagement, reasoning made explicit, justified challenges, alternatives.

## Bales IPA (1950) — 12 categories
- bgiveinfo: gives information/orientation — orients, repeats, clarifies, confirms.
- bgiveopin: gives opinion — evaluates, analyzes, expresses feeling/wish.
- bgivesug: gives suggestion/direction — proposes a course of action (others stay autonomous).
- baskinfo: asks for information/orientation.
- baskopin: asks for opinion/evaluation/analysis.
- basksug: asks for suggestion/direction/possible action.
- bsolid: shows solidarity — raises others' status, helps, praises, encourages.
- btension: shows tension release — jokes, laughs, shows satisfaction.
- bagree: agrees — passive acceptance, understands, concurs, complies.
- bdisagree: disagrees — passive rejection, formality, withholds help.
- btensh: shows tension — asks for help, withdraws, anxiety.
- bantag: shows antagonism — deflates others, defends/asserts self aggressively.

## ISO 24617-2 dialogue acts (5 functions)
- qset: set/WH question — open question requesting info to fill a gap (quem/o que/como/por que…).
- qprop: propositional/polar (yes-no) question.
- directive: directive — request/instruct/suggest the addressee perform an action.
- commissive: commissive — offer/promise/commit the speaker to a future action.
- feedback: feedback/backchannel — acknowledgments ("ok","uhum","entendi"), signaling attention/understanding.

## act4teams-SHORT (Klünder et al. 2020) — 11 categories
- namep: naming problems — identifying/explaining a problem.
- linkp: linking problems — analyzing problem causes/consequences.
- names: naming solutions — gathering/elaborating solutions.
- links: linking solutions — analyzing requirements, comparing solutions.
- linkc: linking & connecting — showing links between problems and solutions.
- cprod: counterproductivity — backbiting, putting others down, complaining, blaming.
- proact: proactivity — interest in ideas, engagement, taking responsibility.
- struct: structuring — prioritizing, procedural suggestions, allocating tasks/roles, summarizing.
- ginfo: giving information — passing on information / external sources.
- ktrans: knowledge transfer — applying own knowledge, explaining information.
- coop: cooperation — praising, thanking, being nice.
